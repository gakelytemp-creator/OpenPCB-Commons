# Founding Principles

OpenPCB Commons begins from a simple observation:

**Owning an object should not require remaining ignorant of it.**

We are a small minority of users who want more than consumption. We want to understand, repair, modify, reuse, teach from, and sometimes completely repurpose the devices around us.

This project exists for that minority — and for anyone who becomes curious later.

## 1. Understanding is a legitimate use

An electronic object is not only a consumer product. It is also a physical record of engineering decisions.

We consider careful investigation, measurement, reverse engineering, documentation, criticism, repair, and reuse to be legitimate forms of technical culture.

## 2. Knowledge should carry its provenance

A conclusion is more valuable when the path to it is visible.

Whenever practical, records should preserve:
- what object was studied;
- what instrument or method was used;
- under what conditions;
- the raw observation or measurement;
- the interpretation;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- who contributed it.

**How we know is part of what we know.**

## 3. Incomplete knowledge is still knowledge

No contributor is required to finish a black box.

A photograph, one identified IC, one recovered net, one measurement, one repair case, or one corrected hypothesis can be useful.

The archive should grow by accumulation, not by demanding perfection.

## 4. There should be many ways in

OpenPCB Commons is not defined by one scanner, one probe, one laboratory, or one approved method.

Different instruments may reveal different layers of the same object.

We welcome forks, alternative machines, hand measurements, microscopes, field probes, optical methods, powered tests, passive tests, imaging methods, and techniques not yet invented.

**The more corridors are open, the harder knowledge is to lose.**

## 5. Repair is not the end of usefulness

A product can have a second life beyond its original purpose.

Motors, sensors, displays, printheads, power supplies, actuators, mechanical assemblies, controllers, and other modules can become building blocks for new machines.

We want to record the information that makes this possible.

## 6. Commercial life and technical life are not the same

A manufacturer may stop selling a product while the physical objects continue to exist for decades.

We especially value companies that publish technical information when a product is discontinued or no longer commercially important.

A product that was once a black box can become an open box later.

## 7. Technical criticism is part of technical culture

When an opened design contains an elegant solution, it deserves recognition.

When it contains a weak, wasteful, fragile, unnecessarily proprietary, or simply strange decision, that too may deserve discussion.

Criticism should be open to correction by other contributors and by new evidence.

## 8. Openness should be visible

We intend to maintain records of open boxes and black boxes, repairability, second-life usefulness, documentation availability, and changes over time.

The point is not to preserve enemies. The point is to make openness and closure visible.

## 9. Founded does not mean owned

OpenPCB Commons was founded by Giorgi (`gakelytemp-creator`) in Georgia in 2026.

That records the beginning, not ownership of the future.

If others join, build, measure, correct, fork, archive, and teach, this commons becomes theirs too.

## 10. Leave a road behind you

The simplest rule is this:

**Do not make the next curious person start again in darkness.**

We may be few.

But for every black box, three of us will eventually find the time.
