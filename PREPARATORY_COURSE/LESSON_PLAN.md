# Preparatory Course — Working Lesson Plan

This is a **working map**, not a frozen school syllabus.

The ordering should be revised after experiments with learners. Each topic should be designed through the four views: Discovery, Principles, Formalization, and Applications.

## Stage 0 — Entering the laboratory

- What is measurement?
- Observation versus interpretation
- Tools as extensions of perception
- Voltage, current, resistance: first operational distinctions
- Safety and the difference between simulation and real action

Goal: make instruments and circuits feel approachable rather than ceremonial.

## Stage 1 — Sources, potential, and current

- Materials and charge behavior
- Metals, electrolytes, and chemical sources
- Potential difference
- Current as transport
- Resistance and dissipation
- Simple DC circuits

Primary analogy family: hydraulic pressure, flow, restrictions, reservoirs.

## Stage 2 — Storage and time

- Capacitance
- Charging and discharging
- Energy storage
- Time constants
- Why transient behavior matters

Primary analogy family: elastic chambers, membranes, and compressible storage.

## Stage 3 — Magnetism, induction, and inductance

This is proposed as an early **pilot lesson** because inductance is often remembered as mysterious and formula-first.

Conceptual discovery chain to develop:

1. Let the learner play with a magnet first.
2. Drop a magnet through a thick copper pipe and compare it with a non-magnetic object.
3. Cut the pipe longitudinally: the magnet falls much faster.
4. Replace the continuous pipe with electrically isolated ring-like sections: the slowing effect can reappear because closed circular conducting paths remain.
5. Break the closed paths: the effect weakens or disappears.
6. Transform the ring idea gradually toward a coil.
7. Compare an open coil with a closed coil while the magnet moves through it.
8. Ask the learner what requires a closed path and what moves along that path.
9. Only after this evidence, connect the behavior to induced current, magnetic response, self-induction, and finally inductance.

The lesson should make the learner infer the importance of **closed current paths and change**, rather than receiving "inductors oppose change in current" as an unexplained slogan.

Possible later formalization:
- Faraday and Lenz relations
- inductance
- energy in magnetic fields
- RL transients

Applications:
- relays and solenoids
- transformers
- motors and generators
- switching converters
- filters
- wireless power
- sensing

## Stage 4 — Nonlinearity and one-way behavior

- Diodes
- Junction intuition
- Rectification
- Clamping and protection
- LEDs and photodiodes
- Semiconductor behavior and the limits of the hydraulic analogy

## Stage 5 — Control

- Transistors as controlled elements
- BJT and MOSFET intuition
- Switching versus amplification
- Bias
- Gain
- Power stages

## Stage 6 — Signals and feedback

- AC and frequency
- RC / RL / RLC behavior
- Filters
- Resonance
- Amplification
- Feedback
- Stability and oscillation

## Stage 7 — Power

- Linear regulation
- Switching regulation
- Conversion
- Efficiency
- Protection
- Grounding and return paths
- Real power-supply blocks

## Stage 8 — Digital electronics

- Thresholds and logic
- Gates as physical circuits
- Memory
- Clocks
- Interfaces
- Microcontrollers

## Stage 9 — From idea to board

- Schematic reading
- Functional blocks
- Reusable circuit patterns
- Component selection
- Layout intuition
- Measurement points
- Debugging
- PCB design

This stage should connect directly to OpenPCB Commons circuit-pattern knowledge and real board examples.

## Stage 10 — Open a real box

The learner enters the Commons itself:

- choose a board;
- photograph or inspect it;
- identify one functional block;
- compare it to known circuit patterns;
- make a safe measurement;
- form a hypothesis;
- preserve evidence;
- find one reusable component or second-life path;
- contribute something useful for the next investigator.

## Lesson media package

For each major topic, aim to create a reusable source package from which several presentations can be generated:

- comic / printable pages;
- slides;
- discovery story;
- principle animation or interactive;
- formula sheet / explanation;
- application gallery;
- optional challenges;
- physical experiment notes;
- LLM interaction notes.

This lets the same conceptual work survive across paper, web, video, 3D, and VR.

## Evaluation research

The methodology should be treated as an experiment.

Focus groups can test:

- immediate conceptual understanding;
- curiosity generated;
- ability to explain in the learner's own words;
- transfer to a new problem;
- delayed recall;
- speed of relearning after partial forgetting;
- ability to reconstruct formulas from principles;
- willingness to experiment independently.

The target is not only retention.

The target is **living knowledge**.
